# Prevent, tolerate, detect, repair: a situated decision frontier

**Non-normative research.** This is a semantic projection of [Measured Risk](https://github.com/JeanHuguesRobert/cogentia/blob/main/research/measured_risk.md), [Optimistic Mainline Governance](https://github.com/JeanHuguesRobert/cogentia/blob/main/research/optimistic_mainline_governance.md), and the [provider effect boundary](deferred-effects-provider-commit-boundary.md), not an amendment to COP.

## Correct distinction

An **error** is a mismatch (with intent, evidence, constraints or outcome). A **cost** is an anticipated consequence; an **accepted loss** is a negative outcome within a declared envelope; **damage** harms a protected interest. Some errors cause no damage, some damages occur without a technical error, and an error is never authorized solely by a budget.

Separate:
1. **Prevent** when an act crosses hard rights, lacks mandate, has uncontrolled third-party externalities, discloses confidential matter, destroys irreplaceable data, or has no credible recovery.
2. **Optimistically attempt** when the act is explicitly authorized, small and sufficiently bounded, the expected learning/action value is worthwhile, and the recovery envelope is credible.
3. **Provisional or staged act** if recoverability or consequence propagation is uncertain: sandbox, simulation, draft, reversible pilot, delayed public exposure.
4. **Contain / rectify / restitute / compensate / reconcile** when observed deviation occurs; keep causal trace and distinguish remediable from residual harm.
5. **Abstain or escalate** if crucial dimensions cannot be bounded responsibly. An unknown cost is not zero and does not necessarily mean no action: apply explicit policy, rights and authority.

## Not a single scalar risk score

Decision dimensions remain heterogeneous:
- Mandate, scope, rights of affected parties;
- Exposure, propagation, reversibility, compensability, restitution, repair, residual harms;
- Risk appetite, error/loss budget, option loss, opportunity cost;
- Time to learn, probability and confidence when actually estimable;
- Human attention, compute, financial, coordination/lease costs (observed, estimated or unknown);
- Temporal conflict: current version, stale claim, revocation, effect provider boundary;
- Quality and fairness of correction, contestation and conciliation.

A transaction can be safe for data integrity yet invalid for authority. A compensating transaction can correct a balance without reversing disclosure, rights violations or loss of trust.

## Decision questions (working card)

1. What precisely is being attempted, by whom and under what currently valid authority?
2. What failures are plausible, and whose interests could they affect?
3. Which outcomes *must* be prevented, and why? Can the provider enforce that boundary?
4. Which deviations can be observed promptly and with reliable evidence?
5. What concrete remedy exists, by whom, within what delay and budget? What residue remains?
6. Does the smallest real action generate enough value or learning relative to bounded exposure?
7. If new evidence invalidates the initial hypothesis, can the handler stop, reconcile and leave a truthful receipt?
8. What peripheral observations should remain noticeable, without mandating an A/B experiment?

## Three discriminating examples

| Situation | Default experimental posture | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Small, reversible documentation correction in a live repository | Optimistic version-aware commit, test/trace and successor correction if needed | Limited exposure; cheap correction; no need to freeze all writers |
| Public disclosure of protected data | Prevent before release; human and rights review | Revert of a commit may not undo disclosure or downstream copies |
| Synthetic cross-provider claim with stale authorization | Probe the time-of-check/use boundary, only no-effect simulation until provider guarantees known | Target CAS does not itself reflect mandate revocation |

## Important possible discovered in passing

The proper optimization target might be **effective recoverability of the whole consequence chain**, not merely reversibility of the original technical command. This includes information propagation, reliance by third parties, attention used by remediation and the right of affected persons to contest the proposed remedy.

A system may discover and repair technical discrepancies faster than existing institutions yet still fail on human rights, consent, fairness or actual restoration. Claims of global improvement require measured comparisons and must not be assumed.

## Next normal-course observation

When an actual low-Exposure change or synthetic conflict naturally arises, record whether preventive safeguards, optimistic execution, or a staged pilot would have changed the result; include attention and repair costs. No dedicated A/B campaign is required. Do not generalize from one success.
